## [Why AI Hasn't Replaced Software Engineers, and Won't](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Jun/14/why-ai-hasnt-replaced-software-engineers/#atom-everything) ⭐️ 7.0/10

Princeton researchers Arvind Narayanan and Sayash Kapoor published an essay arguing with empirical data that AI is not causing mass software engineer layoffs, citing that out of 160+ New York WARN Act filings in the first year of an AI disclosure checkbox (added March 2025), not a single company attributed layoffs to AI. They conclude that if even software engineering—a profession uniquely suited to AI disruption with few regulatory barriers—is resisting automation, most other professions will be even more insulated. This piece directly counters the prevailing narrative of imminent AI-driven mass unemployment with hard data, providing reassurance to knowledge workers and a more grounded framework for policy discussions. It also reframes what software engineering actually is—not just typing code, but deciding what to build, verifying outcomes, and applying deep contextual understanding—which has implications for how teams should integrate AI tools. The authors identify three real bottlenecks in software engineering that resist automation: (1) deciding and specifying what to build, (2) verifying and being accountable for delivered work, and (3) the deep human understanding of codebase, business, and environment needed for both. Simon Willison adds that while AI assists with deciding and verifying, the value of an engineer ultimately depends on how deeply they understand the problems and solutions agents are working on.

rss · Simon Willison · Jun 14, 23:54

**Background**: Arvind Narayanan and Sayash Kapoor are Princeton researchers and co-authors of the 2024 book 'AI Snake Oil', which critically examines AI hype versus reality. The U.S. WARN Act requires companies with 100+ employees to give 60 days' notice before mass layoffs; New York became the first state to add an AI-related cause checkbox to these filings in March 2025, following Governor Kathy Hochul's directive. Simon Willison, who curated this link, is a prominent software engineer and creator of Datasette who frequently blogs about practical AI use in development.
