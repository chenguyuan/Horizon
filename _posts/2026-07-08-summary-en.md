## [EU Mandates Driver Monitoring Cameras in All New Cars from July 2026](https://allaboutcookies.org/eu-mandatory-distracted-driver-system) ⭐️ 7.0/10

Under the EU's General Safety Regulation (GSR), all newly registered passenger vehicles, vans, trucks, and buses sold in the European Union must include an Advanced Driver Distraction Warning (ADDW) system — typically a camera-based Driver Monitoring System (DMS) — starting July 2026. The technology tracks gaze direction, head movements, blinking, and attention levels to alert drivers who appear drowsy or distracted. The mandate will affect an estimated 18 million European cars and reshape the in-cabin experience for every new car buyer, while raising significant concerns about surveillance, data privacy, and driver autonomy. It also signals a broader regulatory push toward always-on biometric monitoring in consumer products, which may extend to other jurisdictions and device categories. The system, known as ADDW, is part of the final phase of the updated GSR and uses AI-powered neural networks to analyze eye movement, blinking speed, and gaze in real time; suppliers like Mobileye and Smart Eye already fuse this with ADAS cameras for road context. Driver monitoring is not new — Toyota introduced the first DMS in 2006 — but this is the first blanket mandate covering all vehicle categories.

hackernews · nickslaughter02 · Jul 7, 20:50 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48823557)

**Background**: The EU General Safety Regulation (GSR) is a sweeping vehicle safety framework that has been phased in since 2022, requiring features like intelligent speed assistance, emergency lane-keeping, and event data recorders in new vehicles. Driver Monitoring Systems (DMS) use infrared or visible-light cameras aimed at the driver's face to infer attention state via computer vision, typically without recording or transmitting footage externally. The ADDW requirement specifically targets distraction (as opposed to earlier DDAW rules focused on drowsiness) and applies to all newly registered vehicles from July 2026, following an earlier phase-in for new vehicle type approvals.

## [Microsoft Reportedly Lays Off idTech Engine Team at id Software](https://gamefromscratch.com/microsoft-fire-idtech-team-at-id-software/) ⭐️ 7.0/10

According to reports, Microsoft has laid off the idTech engine team at id Software on the same day as a Doom: The Dark Ages DLC launch, effectively ending in-house development of the 30-year-old proprietary engine lineage. The move suggests future id Software titles may migrate to Unreal Engine 5 or another third-party engine. idTech is one of gaming's most historically significant engine lineages, having pioneered 3D graphics through Doom, Quake, and Wolfenstein, so its demise marks a symbolic milestone in the consolidation of the industry around Epic's Unreal Engine. It also reflects a broader trend of major publishers abandoning proprietary technology in favor of commodity engines and interchangeable contractor labor. The layoffs are part of a larger wave of Microsoft gaming cuts following its $7.5 billion 2020 acquisition of ZeniMax Media (id Software's parent), and some commenters caution that the article lacks concrete evidence identifying which specific coders were let go. Notably, unlike previous idTech generations, idTech 5 and later versions were never open-sourced, so if the team is fully disbanded the codebase may be permanently locked away rather than passed to the community.

hackernews · bauc · Jul 7, 15:33 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48819244)

**Background**: id Software, founded by John Carmack and John Romero, pioneered the first-person shooter genre and developed a series of successive game engines (idTech 1 through 7) that were widely licensed to other studios and often released as open source, influencing derivatives like Valve's GoldSrc and Infinity Ward's IW engine. Microsoft acquired id Software indirectly through its 2020 purchase of ZeniMax Media (Bethesda's parent company) for $7.5 billion, one of the largest deals in gaming history. In recent years idTech has faced growing pressure from Unreal Engine, which has become the dominant licensed engine across the AAA industry due to its rich tooling and large contractor talent pool.

## [sqlite-utils 4.0 released with schema migrations and nested transactions](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Jul/7/sqlite-utils-4/#atom-everything) ⭐️ 7.0/10

Simon Willison released sqlite-utils 4.0, the first major version bump since 3.0 in November 2020, introducing three headline features: Python-based database schema migrations, nested transactions via a new db.atomic() method, and support for compound foreign keys. The release is the 124th for the project and includes some small but significant breaking changes documented in an upgrade guide. sqlite-utils is a widely-used Python library and CLI tool for working with SQLite, so adding first-class schema migrations closes a major gap that previously forced developers to reach for tools like Alembic or hand-rolled scripts. The addition of nested transactions and compound foreign keys also brings sqlite-utils closer to feature parity with heavier ORMs while keeping its lightweight, SQLite-focused ergonomics. Migrations are defined as decorated Python functions in a migrations.py file and applied via `uvx sqlite-utils migrate data.db migrations.py`, leveraging the existing table.transform() method which implements SQLite's officially-recommended pattern of creating a new table, copying data, dropping the old one, and renaming. Because SQLite's native ALTER TABLE is very limited, this transform-based approach lets migrations handle column type changes, renames, and other operations that are otherwise unsupported.

rss · Simon Willison · Jul 7, 19:32

**Background**: sqlite-utils is a Python CLI utility and library created by Simon Willison for manipulating SQLite databases, popular for its ability to pipe JSON, CSV, or TSV data directly into auto-generated SQLite tables. It is part of the broader Datasette ecosystem, an open-source multi-tool for exploring and publishing data via interactive websites and APIs. Schema migrations are a standard practice in application development for versioning and evolving a database's structure over time, ensuring that changes are applied consistently across environments.
